# PartsLoc privacy policy

**Effective date: 2026-11-04**

PartsLoc is a parts and inventory app by the PartsLoc developer, GitHub user OdinKral ("we", "us"). It helps you keep
track of which parts you have and where they are. Questions about this policy:
odinkral@gmail.com.

**In short:** your parts, counts, places and photos live on your phone. The app has no
account, no ads and no tracking, and it does not send your inventory to us or to anyone
else.

## What the app stores, and where

- **On your phone only.** Your parts, places, counts, history and notes are stored in a
  database on your phone. The name you may type in Settings (it labels who changed what in
  the history) is stored in the app's settings file. Photos of parts are saved as picture
  files in the app's private storage. The app does not add encryption of its own; it
  relies on your phone's lock screen and storage protection.
- **We do not receive it.** The app has no server of ours built in. We do not run an
  account system, and we cannot see your data.
- **Android backup.** If your phone's backup is turned on, Android may copy the parts
  database and the part photos to your Google account, so your inventory survives a change
  of phone. That copying is done by Android and Google, not by us, and you control it in
  your phone's backup settings. The app's settings file (your name and theme) is left out
  of that backup.

## The camera, and your part photos

The camera is used for two things: scanning QR codes and barcodes (to find a part or a
shelf), and taking photos of a part. Scanning is done on the phone.

A part can have several photos, each with a note. You can take a photo with the camera or
choose one from your gallery. Before the app keeps a photo it re-saves it: it is turned
upright, shrunk to at most 1600 pixels on its long side, and written **without any of the
information a phone puts in a picture, including location (GPS), date, time and phone
model**. The camera's temporary copy is deleted once that is done. A picture you chose from
your gallery is not changed or moved. The app has no location permission at all.

Part photos stay on the phone that took them. They are not uploaded anywhere. They are not
included in the file you can share to merge two phones' inventories (only each photo's
note and its place in the part's set are in the app's own backup file), so in this version
photos do not travel from one phone to another. Deleting a part deletes its photos.

## Labels and printing

The app can make a printable label (a QR code and the part's name or place) and hand it to
Android's print system. Which printer or print service receives it is your choice. We never
see the label.

## Advertising and analytics

The app has no ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no tracking code of our
own, and we do not sell or share your data.

## Google's ML Kit

Scanning codes uses Google's ML Kit library, which does the recognition on the phone. ML
Kit is Google's software, not ours. Google's documentation says ML Kit may send technical
information such as device and app details to Google to keep the library working, and that
is governed by Google's own terms and privacy policy. We cannot see or control it, and we do
not receive it.

## Optional server

The version of PartsLoc on Google Play has no server settings and does not connect to any
server for your inventory. (Other builds of the same app that a workplace may give its own
staff can connect to a server that workplace runs. They are not distributed on Google Play,
and that server's owner's policy applies to them.)

## Files you share, files you open, and links

- Backups, spreadsheets and the file for merging two phones leave the phone only when you
  pick a place to send them with the phone's share menu, and only to the place you pick.
- Files you give the app (a spreadsheet you import, or a file another phone shares to
  PartsLoc) are read on the phone. The app shows what would change before it changes
  anything.
- If you give a part a supplier web address and tap it, your phone opens it in its web
  browser, and that website's own policy applies.
- If a part has a **photo web address** (it can come from an imported spreadsheet or
  backup), the app downloads that picture itself when you open the part. That tells the
  website your phone's internet address. Do not import spreadsheets whose picture links you
  do not trust.

## Permissions

The app asks for: **Camera** (scanning codes and taking part photos), **Internet** (only to
download a picture from a photo web address, as described above, and by Google's ML Kit),
and **network state** (to know whether the phone is online). It does not ask for your
location, contacts, microphone, or access to your files and photos in general; picking a
photo or file goes through Android's own picker, which hands the app only what you choose.

## Deleting your data

Open your phone's Settings, find PartsLoc, and choose Clear storage (or uninstall the app).
Everything the app kept on the phone is removed. Make a backup first if you want to keep
it. Copies you already shared, and any Android backup copy of the database and photos made
by Google, are not removed by this; delete those where they live (your Google account's
backup settings).

## Children

PartsLoc is a work tool and is not directed at children.
